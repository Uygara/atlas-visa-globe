    How the 90 / 180 rule actually works

    Under Regulation (EU) 2018/1806, short-stay travellers in the Schengen Area may stay a maximum of 90 days within any rolling 180-day window. The window is not a fixed calendar period: each day you are physically in Schengen you "spend" one of your 90 days; that day will be "refunded" exactly 180 days later.

    This calculator counts every day between entry and exit inclusive (the rule treats partial days as full days). It is intended for travellers on a short-stay Schengen visa or visa-exempt nationals visiting Schengen — it does not apply to long-stay national visas or residence permits.

    Schengen members covered: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland.

    What about UK, Ireland, EU residency cards?

    The UK and Ireland are not in Schengen — time spent there doesn't count toward the 90/180 limit. Holding an EU residence card (e.g. Spain Non-Lucrative, Portugal D7) means you live there legally and aren't subject to the short-stay rule for that country, but days in other Schengen countries still count.
