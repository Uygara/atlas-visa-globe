How airport transit visas work. Most countries distinguish between airside transit (you stay in the international zone, don't pass immigration) and landside transit (you exit the terminal, e.g. to switch terminals or re-check bags). Schengen, UK and a handful of others require a transit visa for certain nationalities even airside. The US and Canada don't have an airside option at all — every transit means clearing immigration.

Exemptions usually exist. If you hold a valid visa or residence permit for the US, UK, Canada, Schengen, Australia or Japan, you'll often qualify for an exemption that lets you transit without a separate transit visa. Tick the boxes above to see whether yours applies.

TWOV (Transit Without Visa) programs exist in Japan (72h), Singapore (96h), Hong Kong (7 days for most), China (144h at major airports). These can let you exit the airport during a long layover — eligibility depends on your nationality and your onward ticket.
