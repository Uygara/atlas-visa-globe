Airport transit visa checker

Do you need a visa to change planes in a third country? Schengen, UK, US and Canada have rules that catch travellers every week, costing flights, hotels and rebooking fees. Pick your passport, your transit hub, and see if you're cleared.

How airport transit visas work. Most countries distinguish between airside transit (you stay in the international zone, don't pass immigration) and landside transit (you exit the terminal, e.g. to switch terminals or re-check bags). Schengen, UK and a handful of others require a transit visa for certain nationalities even airside. The US and Canada don't have an airside option at all: every transit means clearing immigration.

Schengen is not one rule. Twelve nationalities need an airport transit visa at every Schengen airport (EU Visa Code, Annex IV). Single states add more nationalities to their own lists: Turkish citizens, for example, need one in Germany, the Netherlands, France, Belgium, Spain, the Czech Republic, Switzerland and Norway, but not in Italy, Austria or Greece. We read the official list (Visa Code Handbook, Annex 7B) and show the rule for each airport.

Exemptions. For Schengen, a Schengen visa or residence permit, a visa of Ireland, Cyprus, the US, Canada or Japan, and the residence permits listed in the Visa Code lift the requirement. A UK or Australian visa does not. For the UK, a visa or permanent residence of the US, Canada, Australia or New Zealand, an EEA long-stay visa or residence permit and an Irish visa do. Tick the boxes above to check yours.

TWOV (transit without visa) programs exist in Japan (72h), Singapore (96h), Hong Kong (7 days for most), China (240h at designated ports). These can let you exit the airport during a long layover: eligibility depends on your nationality and your onward ticket.

Lists come from each country's official source, linked in every answer, and are checked every day by our data audit. Open an issue at github.com/Uygara/atlas-visa-globe if you spot something wrong or out of date.
