ETIAS in 2026: who needs it, and how it differs from a visa

By Uygar Atalay · Updated 17 June 2026 · about a 5-minute read

ETIAS is the European Travel Information and Authorisation System — a new online registration that many visa-free visitors will need before entering most of Europe. It has been talked about for years and repeatedly delayed; this guide explains what it is, who it affects, and the important fact that it is not a visa.

What ETIAS is

ETIAS is a pre-travel authorization for people who can already enter the Schengen Area without a visa. Instead of applying at an embassy, you fill in a short online form with your passport details, background questions and travel plans. The system runs automated security checks and, in the large majority of cases, returns an approval within minutes. That approval is electronically linked to your passport — there is nothing to print or stick in your passport.

ETIAS is not a visa. It does not grant any new right of entry; it is a pre-screening of travellers who are already visa-exempt. Border officers still make the final decision, and the 90-days-in-180 limit still applies on top of it.

Who needs it

ETIAS applies to nationals of countries that are currently visa-free for short stays in the Schengen Area — for example travellers from the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Brazil, Japan and dozens of others. If your passport already needs a Schengen visa, nothing changes: you keep applying for the visa, and ETIAS does not apply to you. If your passport is visa-free today, ETIAS is the new step you add.

Who is exempt

  • Citizens of EU and Schengen countries.
  • Holders of a Schengen residence permit or long-stay national visa.
  • People who hold a short-stay Schengen visa (they are already screened through the visa process).

Cost, validity and timing

ETIAS is designed to be cheap and long-lasting: a small one-off fee (with under-18s and over-70s exempt from the fee), valid for several years or until your passport expires — whichever comes first. Within that validity you can use it for multiple trips. Because most approvals are near-instant but some are referred for manual review that can take days, the advice is to apply well before you book, not the night before you fly.

How to avoid the scams

Whenever a new travel-authorization system launches, lookalike websites appear charging inflated "service" fees. Apply only through the official EU ETIAS portal. Any site charging many times the official fee, or asking for payment before the system is officially live, should be treated with suspicion.

ETIAS, ETA, ESTA — keeping them straight

These all belong to the same family: electronic authorizations for visa-free travellers. ESTA is the United States version, eTA is Canada's, the UK has launched its own ETA, and ETIAS is Europe's. They work the same way — register online, get approval tied to your passport, board your flight — but each is separate and only valid for its own region. Frequently asked questions

Is ETIAS live now?

Its start has been planned and postponed several times. Check the official EU timeline for the current launch date, and use our ETIAS checker to confirm whether your nationality is in scope.

Does ETIAS replace the Schengen visa?

No. It only applies to people who are already visa-free. Visa-required nationalities keep using the normal visa.

How long can I stay with ETIAS?

It does not change your allowance — the usual 90 days in any 180-day period still applies. See the 90/180 guide.
