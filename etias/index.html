    ETIAS: Europe's new travel authorization

    ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorisation System) is an online travel authorization that visa-exempt travellers will need for short stays in 30 European countries: the 29 Schengen states and Cyprus. It is not in operation yet, and the EU has not announced a start date; it says it will publish the date several months ahead. When it starts it costs €20 and is valid for three years or until your passport expires.

    Who's affected

    If your country's passport currently lets you enter Schengen visa-free for short stays (and you're not already an EU / EEA / Swiss citizen), ETIAS will apply to you once it starts. The affected nationalities include: United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Mexico, Israel, Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, UAE, Albania, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Bosnia & Herzegovina, and most Caribbean and South-Pacific states. Use the checker above for your specific passport.

    What's NOT covered by ETIAS

    • EU / EEA / Swiss citizens: freedom of movement applies; nothing changes.
    • Travellers who already need a Schengen visa (Turkey, India, China, Russia, most African and Middle-Eastern passports): keep applying for visas as usual.
    • Long-stay (more than 90 days): that's a national visa or residence permit, not ETIAS.
    • Work / study / family reunification: full visa categories, not touched by ETIAS.
    • Ireland: not one of the 30 ETIAS countries; no ETIAS needed for Ireland.

    How to apply (when launched)

    1. Open the official ETIAS portal: travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en. Beware of scam sites: only use the .europa.eu domain.
    2. Fill in passport details, travel plans, criminal history questions.
    3. Pay the €20 fee with a payment card. Under-18s and over-70s do not pay.
    4. Most applications are processed within minutes. If not, you get a decision within four days, or up to 14 or 30 days if you are asked for more information or an interview.
    5. Authorization is tied to your passport. Renew when the passport expires.

    Common questions

    Is ETIAS a visa? No. It's a pre-travel security authorization, similar to the US ESTA. You still go through normal border control on arrival.

    What if I'm refused? You'll get a reason (criminal record, prior overstay, security concern). You can appeal, or apply for a regular Schengen short-stay visa instead.

    What if I have dual citizenship? Apply with the passport you'll travel on. If one of your passports is EU/EEA, use that: no ETIAS needed.

    What happens at the border? Once ETIAS runs, the border guard checks your passport and documents on arrival. ETIAS does not guarantee entry.

    The start of ETIAS has been postponed several times. The EU says it will announce the date several months before launch; we update this page when it does. Bookmark this page or turn on alerts for updates.
